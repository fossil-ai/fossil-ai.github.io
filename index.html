Faisal Mohammad

Senior Machine Learning Engineer, Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory

EmailGoogle ScholarLinkedInGitHub

I'm a machine learning engineer with ten years of industry experience building perception systems that have to work outside the lab. Since 2019 I've been at the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, a defense research and development center, working on autonomous maritime platforms where clean data is a luxury and the ocean rarely cooperates. Before that, I built radar target recognition at Northrop Grumman.

APL is full of people with Ph.D.s, so as a side quest (and to fit in) I picked one up myself. My research asks what a model actually relies on, and whether that still holds once the data shifts. Recent work measures how much vision–language models trust misleading cues and uses that to choose the right model for a new domain before running it there, isolates which parts of a vessel a classifier really depends on, and retrieves physically plausible vessels for scene-aware augmentation when maritime data is scarce.

Outside of work: tennis, anime, metal and rock, cooking, too much Reddit, and a lot of black coffee.
